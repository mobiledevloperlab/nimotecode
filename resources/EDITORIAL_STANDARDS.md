# Editorial standards for product guides

Root-level product guides are editorial guides, not keyword containers. A reader should be able to decide whether the product fits their task before they reach a download link.

Every substantial revision must:

- begin with a concrete reader situation or decision, rather than a claim about the product;
- state where a simpler tool is the better choice;
- describe a check the reader can perform before acting (for example, confirm the host, branch, test, or diff);
- distinguish documented capability from a promise about an outcome;
- use an actual, relevant product image with an explanatory caption—never a visible replacement note;
- identify external products fairly, link to primary documentation when making comparison claims, and include a verification date where appropriate.

Avoid stock conversion language such as “not just”, “seamless”, “revolutionary”, “unlock”, “game-changing”, and unsupported superlatives. Do not write as an unnamed collective or imply personal testing that did not occur. Prefer a specific limitation, trade-off, or procedure over a feature list.

Before publishing, an editor must review claims against the product documentation, check links and screenshots, and read the page aloud for repetition and generic transitions. Automated checks can catch unfinished media and disallowed stock phrases; they cannot certify prose quality.
